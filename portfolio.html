Manufacturing Production & Downtime Analysis

An interactive Power BI dashboard that evaluates production performance, operational efficiency and downtime, and turns the results into recommendations.

Microsoft ExcelPower BIPower QueryDAX

Project objective

Analyze manufacturing production data to evaluate production performance, operational efficiency and downtime, then identify opportunities to improve manufacturing operations.

  • Analyzed manufacturing production data to evaluate operational efficiency and production performance.
  • Developed an interactive Power BI dashboard to monitor production KPIs and downtime.
  • Performed data cleaning, data modeling and KPI calculations using Power Query and DAX.
  • Identified operational trends and developed data-driven recommendations for manufacturing process improvement.

Dataset & method

  • Source: Excel workbook with 4 sheets (hourly production, daily summary, hourly breakdown, downtime event log), 1 Jul 2022 to 28 Feb 2023.
  • Power Query: cleaning, type fixes, date correction (events dated 2022-01-13 corrected to 2023-01-13), duration in minutes, data-quality log.
  • Data model: Date and Product dimensions related to the fact tables; DAX measures for production, downtime, efficiency and run rates.
  • Dashboard: one report page, six sections switched by sidebar bookmarks, Date and Product slicers and a Reset Filters button.
  • Notes: the column named production_gallons holds units; OEE is not calculated; efficiency means operating time divided by monitored time.

Findings & Recommendations

Answers to five analysis questions and four recommendations, all computed from the data.

  • Lowest days: Dec 16, 2022 (225 units), Oct 20 (301), Jan 2 (312)
  • 49 stops of 30+ min caused 57.6% of event downtime
  • Downtime and efficiency: correlation -0.55
  • Four recommendations: maintenance, downtime, scheduling, efficiency

Key findings

  1. Lowest production days: Dec 16, 2022 (225 units), Oct 20 (301), Jan 2, 2023 (312). 8 of 56 days under 500 units against an average of 1,087, mostly short runs of about one hour.
  2. High downtime: Dec 13 (418 min) and Dec 6 (412 min). December 2022 held 38% of all downtime; downtime is 76-84% of monitored time at 12:00-13:00.
  3. Products: Product 3 made 76% of units (46,041); in liters the gap narrows to 138,123 vs 74,235.
  4. Downtime vs efficiency: inverse relationship (correlation -0.55); output follows run length, not efficiency.
  5. Opportunities: 49 stops over 30 minutes caused 57.6% of event downtime.

Recommendations

Improving Preventive Maintenance

Review the 49 long stops first and record a cause for every stop.

Reducing Production Downtime

Investigate the 12:00-14:00 window; stagger breaks; add checks for the ~850 stops under 2 minutes.

Improving Production Scheduling

Combine short one-hour runs; allow more setup time for 5 L runs.

Enhancing Operational Efficiency

Set efficiency targets by hour and product and track them weekly.

Conclusion

Operators ran about 45% of monitored time, and a small number of long stops, concentrated around midday and in December 2022, account for most downtime. Tracking stop causes and scheduling longer runs are the most direct ways to raise output.
